The right hospital, with a bed, at a cost the family can carry.

MAP-A Care finds the nearest hospital in Albay that can treat the patient, has a bed that is not reported full, and accepts what the family can pay. By ambulance, or by jeepney, van, bus and on foot. If the first hospital turns the patient away, the next two are already on the screen.

Features

Built around the patient, not the map

The nearest hospital is often the wrong one. It may be full, it may lack the service the patient needs, or it may cost more than the family can pay. Every screen below comes from the working app.

The patient

Say what the patient needs

Tell MAP-A Care how the patient is, what care the patient needs, what the family can pay, and how far the patient can walk. It matches that to what each hospital level offers.

  • Five plain-language status levels, chosen by the crew or the family
  • Eight services: isolation, normal delivery, operation, intensive care, newborn intensive care, high-risk pregnancy, dialysis and blood bank
  • Four payment choices, from free or assisted care only to any hospital
  • A setting for patients who walk slowly: older people, late pregnancy, a disability or an injury

The result

The right hospital, and two backups

The first choice comes with its travel time, its level and services, its cost class, and its free beds with the time of the report. Below it are the next two hospitals that fit, with the extra minutes each would cost if the first one turns the patient away.

  • A hospital that has not reported is shown as not reported, never as available
  • For the two most urgent levels the cost filter is lifted, as Republic Act No. 10932 requires
  • Turn-by-turn guidance by ambulance, or jeepney and walking steps for a commuting patient

The gaps

What money, a need or slow walking cost in minutes

MAP-A Care shows how much longer the trip is because of what the family can pay, because the patient needs a particular service, or because the patient walks slowly. These are the minutes a planner can win back.

  • Payment gap: the extra time against the same patient with no cost limit
  • Need gap: the extra time against the same patient with no particular need
  • Mobility gap: the extra time for a slow walker on public transport

Every hospital, and why

No black box

One tap lists all 29 facilities with the reason each one was kept or ruled out: below the level the patient needs, a missing service, no emergency room, reported full, or above the payment class.

  • Stabilize first: when an open emergency room is much nearer than the right hospital for a critical patient, the app says so
  • A one-tap call to 911 on every hospital screen

For the Provincial Health Office, the PDRRMO and LGUs

Planning tools

The same model, turned from one patient to the whole province. Each tool draws on the map and gives a table a planner can use.

  • Catchment map. Which hospital each place would be sent to, for one kind of patient. It is redrawn in milliseconds when a hospital reports full.
  • Beds per person. Beds within reach per 10,000 people, town by town, with bed deserts where no bed the patient can use is in reach.
  • Surge assignment. Typhoon, eruption or road crash: many patients, few beds. Everyone is placed without overfilling a hospital, emergencies first, with the least total travel time.
  • Critical roads. The roads whose closure would cut towns off from care or delay them most, for clearing first after a typhoon or lahar flow.
  • New Malasakit Centers. Where free or assisted care would reach the most people, with a guarantee on how close the choice is to the best possible.
  • Bed dashboard. Free beds and report age by municipality, and a form to enter a report.

Navigation and planning support, not triage.MAP-A Care does not assess the patient and does not replace 911 or the judgment of an ambulance crew. In an emergency, call 911.

Before and after a typhoon

Know which roads to clear first

Each road an ambulance uses on the way to care is closed in turn, and the app ranks the closures by the people they would cut off and the minutes they would add. Roads that no ambulance route uses are skipped, which halves the work and changes nothing in the answer.

MAP-A Care is in development. It has been tested only on synthetic data. No hospital has been contacted; free beds appear only where someone enters a report, and a report lapses after 12 hours.

How it works

From a patient to the right hospital, in six steps

Every step follows a definition in the research paper and was checked against independently written code.

  1. Step 1

    Hospital map

    Each hospital's level, services, emergency room and cost class, from the DOH registry and the DOH hospital classification.

  2. Step 2

    Patient profile

    How the patient is, what care the patient needs, what the family can pay, and how far the patient can walk.

  3. Step 3

    Suitability

    Level, services, emergency room, beds and cost: five checks for every hospital, with the cost check lifted in an emergency.

  4. Step 4

    Search and backups

    An exact shortest-path search on the ambulance roads or the jeepney network stops once the nearest suitable hospitals are found.

  5. Step 5

    Gaps

    The same search repeated without the cost limit, the need or the slow walking shows what each one costs in minutes.

  6. Step 6

    Planning

    Catchments, beds per person, surge assignment, critical roads and new Malasakit Center sites for the whole province.

Data

Open sources only

Every layer can be traced to a public register or a law, so anyone can check the work.

DOH registry
National Health Facility Registry: the 29 hospitals and infirmaries, their licensed beds, ownership and emergency rooms.
DOH (2012)
Hospital classification: the services each level must offer, which give each hospital its service list.
RA 11463
Malasakit Centers Act: every DOH hospital hosts a Malasakit Center, so it is free or assisted care.
RA 10932
Anti-Hospital Deposit Law: no deposit before emergency care, so the cost filter is lifted for emergencies.
MAP-A
Roads, jeepney, van and bus routes, fares and stops for the 18 LGUs, built on OpenStreetMap. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

2020 Census of Population and Housing: town populations for beds per person and coverage.

About the project

Right Hospital, Right Bed, Right Time

A verified network model for mapping hospitals and routing patients in Albay by clinical need, live bed availability, and ability to pay. MAP-A Care is the app of the paper, built on the MAP-A transit atlas.
